What gets in

  • N4: everyday things a learner meets early: common food (ピザ, 天ぷら, ヨーグルト), the home (エアコン, ゴミ箱, ティッシュ), the body, animals a child knows, shop words (本屋 N5, 花屋, レシート), スマホ.
  • N3: daily-life Japanese: school (部活, 入試, 合宿), part-time work (時給, 店長), stations and shops (終電, 最寄り, 閉店, 半額), restaurants (居酒屋, 大盛り, 食券, 焼肉), phones and apps (アプリ, 充電, アカウント).
  • N2: work, articles and the common onomatopoeia the test uses (就活, 内定, 部署, 経理, 通販, ぐるぐる, ばたばた, ぺらぺら).
  • N1: editorial and specialised words (進捗, 所詮, 稼働, 劣化).

Why words are skipped

    __SKIPLIST__

Speech is full of words the test never uses: fillers and sound effects, slang and dialect (ほんま, むずい), and specific dishes, fish and vegetables (まぐろ, しいたけ, 牛丼). So far more are skipped here than in the written rounds.
