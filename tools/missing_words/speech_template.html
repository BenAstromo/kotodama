What gets in

  • N4: everyday things a learner meets early and hears all the time (スマホ, ピザ, おにぎり, 納豆, 土日).
  • N3: daily-life Japanese: school (部活, 入試, 合宿), shops and stations (店長, 終電, 閉店, 花屋), restaurants (居酒屋, 大盛り, 焼肉), phones and apps (アプリ, 充電, アカウント), and everyday food and home words (天ぷら, ヨーグルト, エアコン, ティッシュ, 本屋).
  • N2: work, articles and the common onomatopoeia the test uses (就活, 内定, 部署, 時給, 通販, ぐるぐる, ばたばた), and easy words people rarely say (ゴミ箱, レシート, 冬休み).
  • N1: editorial and specialised words (進捗, 所詮, 稼働, 劣化).

Frequency rule: a word has to be common enough for its level, using the same sizes as the frequency bars (N5 top 800, N4 1,500, N3 3,900, N2 6,000, N1 13,400). A word judged N4 but ranked past #1,500 in speech moves to N3, and on up until it fits; each moved word says so under its meaning.

Why words are skipped

    __SKIPLIST__

Speech is full of words the test never uses: fillers and sound effects, slang and dialect (ほんま, むずい), and specific dishes, fish and vegetables (まぐろ, しいたけ, 牛丼). So far more are skipped here than in the written rounds.
